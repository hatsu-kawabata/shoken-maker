How many people live within 1 km of any point in Japan?

Shoken Maker is a free, no-signup tool that answers that. Draw a circle anywhere on a map of Japan (radius 500 m–3 km) and it returns the residential population, number of households, gender split and 5-year age structure inside the circle — computed from the official 2020 Population Census 500 m grid published by the Statistics Bureau of Japan (e-Stat).

What the data is — and is not

FigureMeaning
PopulationResidential (nighttime) population — where people live, not daytime/worker population
HouseholdsCensus households whose dwelling falls in the grid cells inside the circle
Average ageFrom 5-year bands; central Tokyo/Osaka has a high share of unreported ages, so bands may not sum to the total
Vintage2020 Census (the most recent confirmed figures; the next census is 2025)

Grid cells are counted when their centroid falls inside the circle, so a single 500 m cell is either fully in or fully out. At a 500 m radius that granularity matters; at 1 km and above the error averages out.

Why use census grid data instead of a population API

Japanese census figures are the ones banks and landlords accept in loan applications and store-opening plans. The 500 m grid is the finest official geography published for the whole country, and it is free to redistribute with attribution — which is why this site can be free and open source.
